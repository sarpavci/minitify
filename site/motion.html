minitify. motion
EVERY MOVE, AT TRUE SIZE

Motion that explains
where things came from.

minitify is a floating AppKit panel with nowhere to hide: it sits over your work, so anything that moves has to earn the attention it takes. This is the whole system — eleven behaviours, each one a live demo at the size it ships at, with its beat map, its exact duration, its curve in both CSS and Core Animation, what deliberately stays still, and what happens under Reduce Motion. Nothing here needs a third-party library; every demo names the native API that carries it.

The vocabulary

Five curves and four duration bands. Every feature below uses one of these; nothing is improvised per-screen.

CURVECSSCORE ANIMATIONWHERE IT IS USED
Enter cubic-bezier(0, 0, .2, 1) CAMediaTimingFunction(controlPoints: 0, 0, 0.2, 1) Anything arriving: reveals, popovers, fades in, thumb grow.
Exit cubic-bezier(.4, 0, 1, 1) CAMediaTimingFunction(controlPoints: 0.4, 0, 1, 1) Anything leaving. Always ~60–70% of the matching entrance.
Standard cubic-bezier(.4, 0, .2, 1) CAMediaTimingFunction(controlPoints: 0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) In-place changes: tint, bounce, a value sliding to a new state.
Panel cubic-bezier(.32, .72, 0, 1) CAMediaTimingFunction(controlPoints: 0.32, 0.72, 0, 1) Geometry: window resize, mode switch, queue reveal. Leaves fast, lands soft.
Settle cubic-bezier(.22, 1.1, .36, 1) CASpringAnimation(mass: 1, stiffness: 320, damping: 26) Only where something physical comes to rest: the drag clamp, a released slider thumb.

The bands

A floating overlay is read in a glance. Only the launch story is allowed to take its time.

120–160 msAcknowledgement. Press feedback, hover reveal, a thumb growing under the pointer. Fast enough that it reads as the control responding, not animating.
180–250 msSymbol and state. SF Symbols .replace and .bounce, accent tint, popover open. The macOS default band.
240–380 msGeometry. Window resize, mode switch, queue reveal, the clamp back inside the frame. Anything where the eye has to follow a shape to a new place.
3600 msLaunch only. The mark plays its four beats once while the player connects; nothing else on the page is allowed near this length.

No animation is added at all — the layers are built at the M frame and stay there. The loading state is still communicated, by the text line below the mark.

REDUCE MOTION

04

Seek and volume

While a finger is down there is no animation at all — the thumb is under the pointer, 1:1, or the control feels broken. The only motion is at the two edges: picking up, and letting go.

NSSLIDER

No lift and no spring. The panel still tracks the pointer — that is direct manipulation, not animation — and on release it is clamped into visibleFrame in a single step.

What holds across all eleven. Every duration sits in one of four bands, and 150–350 ms covers everything except the launch story. Every curve is one of five, and the only spring is on the drag clamp. Entrances use the enter curve, exits use the exit curve at 60–70% of the entrance duration. Nothing scales, slides or bounces unless it is either showing you where something came from or confirming that a press landed — which is why selecting a library row gets 120 ms and a flat colour change, while liking a track gets a bounce.

What stays still is the design. The panel's top edge through every resize. The artwork, title and transport through every mode switch. The row under your pointer while you tap its heart. The cursor's line in the terminal. Each of those is a decision not to move something, and together they are what keeps a window that floats over your work from feeling like it is competing with it.

Reduce Motion is a first-class path, not a degradation. SF Symbols effects and NSAnimationContext honour the system setting on their own; the window resizes and the drag clamp need an explicit check of NSWorkspace.shared.accessibilityDisplayShouldReduceMotion. In every case the state change still happens — only the transition is removed, and the looping equaliser is removed outright. Nothing in this page needs a third-party animation library, and nothing in it is carried by JavaScript: on this page the motion is CSS, and in the product it is Core Animation and SF Symbols.
